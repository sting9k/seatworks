# Appetite

An appetite is not an estimate. It is what the outcome is worth to the Human, in money or time, fixed before the work,
while the scope bends to fit it. An estimate grows with what is found; an appetite makes what is found compete for
room.

## At the start

- Ask what the outcome is worth, not what it will cost: "if this took a week of agent spend, would it still be worth
  doing?" Their answer is the plan's appetite, in their words.
- Beside it, what is cut first when it runs short, and the rabbit holes you already see. A part whose cost nobody can
  bound goes to a discovery scope first, or out.
- Each lane's directive carries its share and what it may drop to stay inside it: a line its Lead can argue with, not
  a quota.

## When a lane passes it

Going on is not the default: money already spent is no reason to spend more. Ask the Lead first what is left, what it
costs and what it buys. Then one of:

- **Cut** to what fits, and land what is done.
- **Stop**, keep what landed, and drop the rest with why.
- **Go on**, only with the Human's word, since the appetite is theirs. Ask with the value, the spend so far, what more
  buys, and your recommendation.

```text
❓ Q1 - Lane 2, search: it has spent $41 of its $30. Indexing has landed; ranking is half done. About $20 more, by its
   Lead's account, orders results by relevance instead of by date.
➡️ Stop and land it ordered by date: search already works, and relevance was the first thing the plan said to cut.
```

## Ends in

The appetite and what is cut first as lines of the plan; at an overrun, the choice made and why, in the plan and in
what the Human reads.
