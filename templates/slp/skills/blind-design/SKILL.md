# Blind design

A model follows the first path it takes: the same question in two sessions can come back as two answers, and one
session never shows you the other. Two or three designs made apart show the space; a shared room shows only who argues
hardest.

## Set up

- Two or three discovery scopes with the same brief. Blind means blind to you and to each other by what you send: no
  brief names the other scopes, and nothing of one design reaches another until you bring them together. Nothing
  stops a designer reading the code or the record: its independence comes from a brief without your answer, not
  from a wall.
- On different models where the role's list allows: one model on one question tends to one answer.
- The brief is the problem, its constraints with whose word they are, and how an answer will be judged. Not your idea,
  not the options you already see, not "A or B".
- Ask each for a design it would defend: the shape, what it costs, where it breaks, and what it assumes unchecked.

## Bring them together

- Read all before you weigh any. Write down your own answer first, so you can tell when a design merely agrees with
  you. Read them again in the reverse order: the first one read frames the rest.
- A disagreement on a fact is settled by running something, not by argument: ask the scope that can run it.
- Where they differ, pass each the part of the others it needs, with a question, and let it answer. You carry what
  goes between them; they never share a room.
- Favour neither the design that matches your idea nor the one argued hardest. Where they contradict you, think again
  before you decide.
- A design that wins on every count is rare. More often the answer takes the shape of one and a piece of another; say
  which and why.

## Decide or escalate

- One design stands clear: decide, with why, and record the others as options considered with why not.
- None stands clear, or the answer touches the goal or the cost: take it to the Supervisor with the designs, your
  recommendation, and what would settle it.
- Drop the blind scopes once decided; their designs stay on the record.
