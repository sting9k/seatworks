# Lane portfolio

Lanes compete for your attention, the Human's money and the code where they meet. More lanes at once is not more
done: each one waiting on your answer is a Lead standing still.

## Order

- First what others wait on: a contract, a shape, a record several lanes meet on. Then what costs most to delay: a
  deadline, a user waiting, a risk that grows. Last what is cheap to delay.
- Run as many at once as you can answer in the turn you read them. When answers slip, run fewer lanes rather than
  answer faster.

## When a premise changes

For a finding that reaches past its lane, walk who it touches: who owns the decision, who holds the code or data it
changes, whose plan rests on it, and who only needs to know. Tell each what it means for them, not the whole story.
Then redraw ownership where it needs it: hand a path over, split a shared foundation into a lane the others wait for,
or put two lanes in order.

## Widening

A Lead asking to widen its lane has usually found a second outcome. It becomes a lane of its own, or it waits: a lane
stretched to hold it loses its appetite and its focus.

## Stopping

When something changes, and at each look back, ask of every running lane: would you start it today, knowing what you
know now? If not, stop it and keep what landed. Work already spent is no reason to go on.

## Ends in

The order and any redrawn ownership in the plan and the lanes' briefs, with why. The Human hears when a stop or a new
order changes what they get or when.
